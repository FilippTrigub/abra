# Abra Value Proposition Plan

## Decision

Use this headline direction for the landing page and core marketing message:

> Turn raw expertise into ready-to-post content.

Abra should be positioned around one obvious behavior: after a useful call, voice note, article, or recording, the expert gives it to Abra and gets ready-to-post content back. Do not lead with the number of skills, infrastructure, GPU hosting, or workflow mechanics. Those support the promise, but they are not the promise.

## Dead-simple pain to solution

### The pain

Your best ideas happen while you are working, not while you are staring at a blank LinkedIn draft.

Independent experts say useful things on client calls, in workshops, in sales conversations, in voice notes, and inside rough drafts. Then those ideas disappear because turning them into posts is a separate job.

### The solution

Give Abra the call, note, recording, or rough idea. Abra turns it into ready-to-post content in your voice.

### Simplest possible explanation

> You already said the useful thing. Abra turns it into the post.

### Behavioral trigger

Use Abra whenever one of these happens:

- You finish a client call with a strong insight.
- You record a voice note you do not want to lose.
- You write a rough idea that is not polished enough to post.
- You host a workshop, webinar, or advisory session.
- You answer a sales objection that other buyers should hear.

### Before / after

Before Abra:

> "That was a useful conversation. I should turn it into content later."

After Abra:

> "Drop the call into Abra. Review the posts. Schedule the ones I like."

## Primary ICP

### Independent B2B experts who sell through trust

This ICP combines consultants, coaches, fractional operators, and founder-led expert service businesses into one focused buyer category.

They sell high-consideration services where buyers need to trust the person before they buy. Their best marketing asset is not a content calendar; it is the expertise already present in client calls, advisory sessions, voice notes, sales objections, frameworks, workshops, and half-written ideas.

They know they should publish consistently, especially on LinkedIn, but content creation competes with client delivery, sales, and operations. They do not want a generic AI writer. They want a reliable way to turn their own thinking into credible content without sounding outsourced or robotic.

### Include

- Solo consultants and advisors
- Executive, leadership, and business coaches
- Fractional CMOs, CTOs, COOs, RevOps leaders, and growth operators
- Boutique expert-service founders
- Founder-led agencies where the founder's point of view drives demand

### Exclude for now

- Broad consumer creators
- Indie hackers with low willingness to pay
- Enterprise social media teams
- Creator teams that mainly need video clipping volume
- Agencies needing multi-client approval workflows as the primary use case
- Developer advocates whose budget and buying process sit inside a company

## Core job to be done

When I finish a useful call, voice note, article, or recording, I want Abra to turn it into credible posts in my voice, so I can stay visible without starting from a blank page.

## Main pain

The customer has enough expertise. The bottleneck is turning it into posts.

They experience the problem as:

- "I said something useful on a call, but it disappeared."
- "I have ideas all week, but I do not turn them into posts."
- "Every LinkedIn post starts from a blank page."
- "ChatGPT gives me generic posts that do not sound like me."
- "A ghostwriter still needs too much context."
- "I know I should publish, but content creation becomes another job."

## Desired outcome

The customer wants a simple capture-to-post habit: finish useful work, send the raw material to Abra, review the posts, schedule the good ones.

The outcome is not "more posts." The outcome is:

- useful ideas stop disappearing
- LinkedIn no longer starts from a blank page
- content sounds like the expert, not a generic AI writer
- expertise becomes visible before sales calls
- publishing becomes a habit attached to work they already do

Abra is a content capture engine for independent B2B experts. It turns useful calls, notes, recordings, and rough ideas into social posts that sound like the expert and are ready to review, edit, and schedule.

## Minimal value proposition

Abra turns the useful things you already say into ready-to-post content that sounds like you.

## Expanded value proposition

Drop in a call recording, voice note, article, or rough idea. Abra finds the useful parts, writes the posts in your voice, creates the supporting media, and prepares everything for review and scheduling.

## Style guide

This style guide describes the intended Abra product and landing-page feeling. It is not a snapshot of the current implementation. Current CSS classes, exact component choices, and section order can change as long as the experience keeps this direction.

### Style thesis

Abra should feel like a calm content operator for serious experts: clear, warm, trustworthy, and quietly capable. The product should not feel like an AI toy, a creator gimmick, or a developer toolkit. It should feel like the place an expert sends useful raw material after a call and gets credible drafts back.

The visual language should make the user feel:

- "This understands my work."
- "This will not make me sound generic."
- "I stay in control before anything goes live."
- "This is simple enough to become a habit."

### Voice and tone

Write like a sharp operator explaining the obvious next step. Use plain sentences, concrete nouns, and human work moments. Prefer "call," "note," "recording," "draft," "post," and "review" over abstract SaaS language.

Tone should be:

- calm, not hype-driven
- specific, not clever for its own sake
- confident, not grandiose
- useful, not inspirational
- reputation-aware, not automation-obsessed

Avoid language that makes Abra sound like it is replacing the expert. Abra prepares, drafts, extracts, organizes, and schedules. The expert reviews, edits, approves, and stays accountable.

### Visual character

The intended look is editorial SaaS for expert services: generous whitespace, strong typography, warm neutral surfaces, soft depth, and a few deliberate brand accents. It should feel premium because it is restrained, not because it is decorative.

Use:

- large, clear headlines with high contrast
- short blocks of supporting copy
- rounded cards and panels with subtle borders
- warm off-white and muted neutral backgrounds
- one strong brand accent for primary actions and key markers
- soft shadows only when they clarify hierarchy
- simple lists, before/after cards, and step cards

Avoid:

- dense feature grids
- icon-heavy sections
- emoji or novelty visual language
- heavy gradients as the main design device
- glassmorphism as a default surface
- dashboard screenshots before the promise is understood
- over-explaining the skill system visually

### Layout principles

Every page should have one obvious job. The landing page's job is to make the capture-to-post habit feel inevitable. Do not introduce multiple competing mental models.

Preferred structure:

1. State the outcome in the hero.
2. Anchor the problem in real work moments.
3. Show the simple input -> draft -> review flow.
4. Address control and voice quality.
5. Repeat one clear CTA.

Keep sections shallow. If a section needs more than a few cards or a paragraph, it probably belongs deeper in the product, docs, or onboarding.

### Component and interaction principles

Buttons should be large enough to feel easy and deliberate. Primary CTAs should use one clear action phrase, preferably "Create my first content batch." Secondary actions should explain, not compete.

Cards should read like useful notes, not decoration. Use them for triggers, before/after contrasts, steps, and trust points. Do not use cards to list every capability.

Navigation should stay quiet on marketing pages. The navbar should support recognition and sign-in; it should not become a second sales section.

Interactions should be subtle: visible focus states, clear hover states, no motion that distracts from reading. Accessibility and touch targets matter because simplicity is part of the promise.

### Product surface style

Inside the app, keep the same calm operator feeling but allow more density where work happens. Dashboard, settings, and deployment surfaces can use panels, badges, status banners, and forms, but they should still feel organized and plain-spoken.

The product UI should prioritize:

- clear next actions
- visible review and approval moments
- status without alarmism
- source material and generated output side by side where possible
- plain labels over internal orchestration language

### Design system alignment

Use the existing platform style system as the implementation base:

- display typography for decisive headlines
- readable body typography with generous line height
- warm brand color for primary action and important markers
- secondary/accent colors sparingly for support states
- warm neutral surface and content tokens for hierarchy
- rounded radii and soft shadows for approachable depth
- focus rings and validation states from the shared primitives

These tokens support the style; they are not the style itself. The style is the user feeling: expert, simple, credible, controlled.

### Examples vs principles

The current landing page is an expression of this guide, not the permanent template. The exact three-step flow, CTA placement, card count, and Tailwind classes can change. Preserve the principles: one promise, one habit, concrete work moments, human review, and restrained visual confidence.

## Strategic notes

- LinkedIn should be the first named channel in marketing because the ICP sells through professional trust.
- Instagram, X, and other platforms can remain secondary proof of distribution breadth.
- The landing page should sell the outcome before explaining the modular skill system.
- The product should feel like a content operator for experts, not a toolkit for engineers.
- Human review should be framed as a strength because this ICP is reputation-sensitive.
- The simplest demo should be: upload one call recording or voice note, get several post drafts back, approve one, schedule it.
