# Product

## Register

product

## Users

### Primary users

Independent technical professionals and expert-led operators who sell through trust.

This includes technical consultants, fractional operators, founder-led agencies, researchers, builders, and boutique expert-service founders whose point of view drives demand. They already produce useful raw material in client calls, workshops, sales conversations, voice notes, shipped work, and rough drafts. Their sharpest pain is simple: they know they need to post to stay visible, but they do not have time to turn useful work into credible content.

They are time-poor, reputation-sensitive, and skeptical of generic AI writing. Many also care about inspectable workflows, source access, and the option to self-host because their voice, client context, and working process are trust assets. They do not want content creation to become a second job. They want a fast path from useful work to publishable drafts without sounding outsourced or robotic.

### Secondary users

Trust-selling experts and operators who want Abra managed for them.

This includes consultants, coaches, advisors, and expert-service founders who still need credible drafts from real calls, notes, workshops, and conversations, but prefer not to run the infrastructure themselves. They care about review surfaces, brand assets, workflow setup, and dependable hosted operation. They are important because managed hosting should feel like convenience, not a different product.

## Product Purpose

Abra stands for **Agent de Branding**.

Abra is a content capture and brand-management system for experts who need to keep posting without making content another job.

Its core job is simple: after a useful call, note, workshop, recording, shipped project, or rough idea, the user gives it to Abra and gets reviewable drafts back. Abra exists for the moment when the expert thinks, **I should post this, but I do not have time.** It removes the blank page, stops useful ideas from disappearing, and makes posting part of the work that already happened.

At the system level, Abra orchestrates capture, transcription, drafting, visual generation, adaptation, scheduling, and asset management. It can be self-hosted by users who want control and source access, or run through managed hosting when they want someone else to handle hosting, updates, storage, monitoring, and provider plumbing. At the user level, the promise should feel much simpler: **no time to post? Abra turns the work into the draft.**

Success looks like this:

- useful ideas no longer disappear after calls
- experts stay visible even when they are busy
- content sounds like the expert, not a generic AI assistant
- review happens before publishing, so trust stays intact
- posting no longer feels like a separate job

## Brand Personality

### Three-word personality

Clear, credible, quietly capable.

### Voice and tone

Abra should sound like a sharp operator, not a hype machine.

- calm, not loud
- specific, not clever for its own sake
- confident, not grandiose
- useful, not inspirational
- reputation-aware, not automation-obsessed

Abra should feel like a serious assistant for experts whose work depends on trust. It should never sound like it is replacing the expert or hiding the workflow from them. Abra prepares, drafts, extracts, organizes, and schedules. The expert reviews, edits, approves, and remains accountable.

### Emotional goals

The product should make users feel:

- understood
- in control
- relieved that posting no longer steals extra time
- confident that the output will still sound like them

## Anti-references

Abra should **not** feel like any of the following:

- a generic AI writer that starts from empty prompts
- a creator gimmick built for content volume over credibility
- an autopublishing robot that removes human judgment
- a developer toolkit that treats infrastructure, GPU hosting, or system mechanics as the main value
- an enterprise social-media suite focused on approvals, teams, and process overhead
- a personal-brand product that promises visibility while sounding fake

Avoid letting skill counts, orchestration complexity, hosted control planes, or multi-channel breadth replace the product promise. Self-hosting and source access can build trust because users can inspect and own the workflow, but they are proof points, not the whole story.

## Design Principles

### 1. Start from something real

Abra should begin with real source material: calls, notes, recordings, drafts, objections, frameworks, and rough ideas. The product is strongest when it feels grounded in what the expert already said.

### 2. Keep the expert in control

Human review is a strength, not a concession. The system should help the user move faster while keeping approval, editing, and accountability visible.

### 3. Optimize for trust before scale

The output must feel credible before it feels prolific. Sounding right matters more than producing more.

### 4. Make the next step obvious

Abra should reduce friction and cognitive load. The user should always understand what to give the system, what comes back, and what to do next.

### 5. Attach publishing to existing work

Abra should turn useful work moments into a repeatable content habit. It is not a separate creative ritual. It is the layer that makes existing expertise visible.

### 6. Treat hosting as a choice, not a lock-in

Abra should make both paths clear. Self-hosting and source access give technical users control and trust. Managed hosting gives busy users convenience through hosted operation, updates, storage, monitoring, and provider plumbing. Neither path should obscure the core job: real work becomes drafts the expert can review and approve.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Default to **WCAG AA** across product and marketing surfaces.
- Favor plain language, strong hierarchy, and obvious next actions because the core user is busy and scanning quickly.
- Respect reduced motion and avoid decorative movement that hides meaning or slows comprehension.
- Keep important actions and states visible on mobile as well as desktop, especially because capture and review may happen on the go.
- Never make trust depend on hidden automation. The user should be able to understand what the system did, what came from source material, and what still needs approval.
