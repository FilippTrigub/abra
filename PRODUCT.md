# Product

## Register

product

## Users

### Primary users

Independent B2B experts who sell through trust.

This includes consultants, coaches, fractional operators, boutique expert-service founders, and founder-led agencies whose point of view drives demand. They already produce useful raw material in client calls, workshops, sales conversations, voice notes, and rough drafts. Their sharpest pain is simple: they know they need to post to stay visible, but they do not have time to turn useful work into credible content.

They are time-poor, reputation-sensitive, and skeptical of generic AI writing. They do not want content creation to become a second job. They want a fast path from useful work to publishable drafts without sounding outsourced or robotic.

### Secondary users

Power users and operators who run Abra inside OpenClaw, Hermes, CLI workflows, or the local platform dashboard.

These users care about orchestration, deployment, brand assets, workflow setup, and review surfaces. They are important for implementation and operations, but they are not the core marketing audience.

## Product Purpose

Abra stands for **Agent de Branding**.

Abra is a content capture and brand-management system for experts who need to keep posting without making content another job.

Its core job is simple: after a useful call, note, recording, or rough idea, the user gives it to Abra and gets reviewable drafts back. Abra exists for the moment when the expert thinks, **I should post this, but I do not have time.** It removes the blank page, stops useful ideas from disappearing, and makes posting part of the work that already happened.

At the system level, Abra orchestrates capture, transcription, drafting, visual generation, adaptation, scheduling, and asset management. At the user level, the promise should feel much simpler: **no time to post? Abra turns the work into the draft.**

Success looks like this:

- useful ideas no longer disappear after calls
- experts stay visible even when they are busy
- content sounds like the expert, not a generic AI assistant
- review happens before publishing, so trust stays intact
- posting no longer feels like a separate job

## Brand Personality

### Three-word personality

Clear, credible, quietly capable.

### Voice and tone

Abra should sound like a sharp operator, not a hype machine.

- calm, not loud
- specific, not clever for its own sake
- confident, not grandiose
- useful, not inspirational
- reputation-aware, not automation-obsessed

Abra should feel like a serious assistant for experts whose work depends on trust. It should never sound like it is replacing the expert. Abra prepares, drafts, extracts, organizes, and schedules. The expert reviews, edits, approves, and remains accountable.

### Emotional goals

The product should make users feel:

- understood
- in control
- relieved that posting no longer steals extra time
- confident that the output will still sound like them

## Anti-references

Abra should **not** feel like any of the following:

- a generic AI writer that starts from empty prompts
- a creator gimmick built for content volume over credibility
- an autopublishing robot that removes human judgment
- a developer toolkit that leads with infrastructure, GPU hosting, or system mechanics
- an enterprise social-media suite focused on approvals, teams, and process overhead
- a personal-brand product that promises visibility while sounding fake

Avoid leading with skill counts, orchestration complexity, hosted control planes, or multi-channel breadth. Those are supporting mechanics, not the product promise.

## Design Principles

### 1. Start from something real

Abra should begin with real source material: calls, notes, recordings, drafts, objections, frameworks, and rough ideas. The product is strongest when it feels grounded in what the expert already said.

### 2. Keep the expert in control

Human review is a strength, not a concession. The system should help the user move faster while keeping approval, editing, and accountability visible.

### 3. Optimize for trust before scale

The output must feel credible before it feels prolific. Sounding right matters more than producing more.

### 4. Make the next step obvious

Abra should reduce friction and cognitive load. The user should always understand what to give the system, what comes back, and what to do next.

### 5. Attach publishing to existing work

Abra should turn useful work moments into a repeatable content habit. It is not a separate creative ritual. It is the layer that makes existing expertise visible.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Default to **WCAG AA** across product and marketing surfaces.
- Favor plain language, strong hierarchy, and obvious next actions because the core user is busy and scanning quickly.
- Respect reduced motion and avoid decorative movement that hides meaning or slows comprehension.
- Keep important actions and states visible on mobile as well as desktop, especially because capture and review may happen on the go.
- Never make trust depend on hidden automation. The user should be able to understand what the system did, what came from source material, and what still needs approval.
